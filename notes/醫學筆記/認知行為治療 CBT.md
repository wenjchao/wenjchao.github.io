**For each irrational belief or thought, help your client think along the following lines:**

- Is there any practical basis for this belief? Why do you feel this is true?

- Does this thought encourage mental stability or personal growth?

- Does this thought stifle your problem-solving ability and thinking to the point where you feel immobilised?

- Does this belief result in self-defeating behaviour? Are you able to use this belief to honestly connect with others?

diary: the client will be instructed to make a note of their negative thoughts, how they felt as a result and the impact this had on their behaviours.

Sometimes, a client will acknowledge that they hold a particular belief, but deny that it is irrational. At this point, a therapist will use careful questions that help a client realise that their beliefs are maladaptive and not based in reality.

\***For instance:*** *If a client insists that “everyone” hates them and that they are intrinsically unlikeable, a therapist may ask them questions such as:
• “Is it really true that absolutely everyone hates you?”
• “How would you prove that everyone hates you?”
• “I don’t hate you and no-one in this building does. May I suggest that your thought is not literally true?”*

Goals should be SMART – Specific, Measurable , Achievable, Relevant and Timely.

- **S – Specific.** Setting a clear, specific goal rather than a vague aspiration. For example, “Go out with friends at least once a week.” rather than “Go out with friends more often.”

- **M – Measurable.** Setting goals that can easily be tracked in terms of their progress.

- **A – Achievable.** Setting goals that can reasonably be achieved. It is discouraging to set over-ambitious goals that cannot be achieved. For example, “Go out with friends every day.” wouldn’t be financially or practicably achievable.

- **R – Relevant.** Setting goals that are relevant to the client and will help to resolve their specific issues. For example, a goal like “Learn a new language.” wouldn’t be particularly helpful for Sandra.

- **T – Time-bound.** Setting goals which are to be reached within a certain time-frame. A client will be more motivated if they feel they can expect results after a reasonable amount of time.

example:

*“To rebuild my social life. Specifically, I want to go out at least once a week with friends.”“To regain the confidence I need to go back to work.
